# code-court-card-transit-adaptations

**Type:** Code Enhancement
**Originated by:** Carmack, Miyazaki, Taleb

**User guidance:** Go deep on tarot itself — the cards' classical Rider–Waite–Smith symbolism, how meanings shift by position in a spread, card-to-card combinations, and how experienced readers weave cards into a single narrative rather than disconnected mini-readings. Then go deeper on the bridge to astrology: every card has planetary, elemental, and zodiacal correspondences, and the real insight lives in how those correspondences land against this user's natal chart and against the user's transits today and through the week ahead. Distill ruthlessly — show only the few connections that actually illuminate this person's question at this moment, said in a way that lands. Prefer one true thing the user didn't already know over five plausible things they did.

---

## Problem / Opportunity

`src/data/tarot/transitAdaptations.ts` contains 56 transit adaptation entries covering 32 distinct card IDs. Of the 78-card deck, 44 Minor Arcana cards have zero entries. All 16 court cards — the four Pages, four Knights, four Queens, and four Kings — are absent from `TRANSIT_ADAPTATIONS_BY_CARD` entirely. A lookup on `page-of-wands`, `queen-of-swords`, `king-of-pentacles`, or any other court card returns `undefined`.

The consequence runs through `buildTransitInfluence` in `src/engine/tarot.ts`. The function implements three fallback levels. Level 1 is a specific transit adaptation lookup. With zero court card entries, every court card draw fails Level 1 regardless of what transit is active — whether Saturn is conjunct a natal angle, whether Pluto is in a generational transit, whether Uranus is mid-station. The function falls to Level 2: a generic element-affinity template that produces output like `"The current Saturn transit amplifies this card's Earth nature — material mastery and prosperity are heightened now."` This sentence will be generated identically for the King of Pentacles, the Knight of Pentacles, the Queen of Pentacles, and the Page of Pentacles, because all four share the Earth element. The King of Wands, the Knight of Wands, the Queen of Wands, and the Page of Wands all receive the same Fire-element sentence under the same transit, regardless of how completely different these four figures are from one another symbolically.

Court cards are the most contextually sensitive cards in the deck. They do not describe events or qualities; they describe people, inner figures, and relational archetypes. The meaning of a court card is radically altered by who it represents — oneself, another person, or an emerging aspect of character — and that determination is exactly what a transit is best positioned to inform. A Saturn transit changes whether the King of Pentacles is someone who has built something solid and can be trusted, or a figure whose material control has calcified into a prison. A Pluto transit changes whether the Queen of Swords is clarity in the service of liberation, or clarity being used to excavate something that has been buried too long. A Uranus transit changes whether the Knight of Wands is creative momentum or chaos that cannot be steered. None of these distinctions are accessible through the Level 2 element-affinity template, which cannot differentiate between card identities that share an element.

The practical miss rate is not marginal. Court cards appear in approximately 20% of any sufficiently large random draw sample (16 of 78 cards). In a Celtic Cross reading, where 10 cards are drawn, court cards will appear in roughly two positions on average. Each court card in every reading currently generates a Level 2 template regardless of what transit conditions are present — the transit signal is received by the engine and then discarded at the data layer before it can be interpreted.

The Level 2 template is also the input the GPT synthesis handlers receive as `transitInfluence` for those cards. `handleTarotSingleSynthesis`, `handleTarotThreeSynthesis`, and `handleTarotCelticSynthesis` in `server/services/gptTarot.ts` all include the transit influence string in the payload sent to the model. When that string is a generic element template, the synthesis cannot do better than repeat or lightly rephrase it — the signal arriving at the model is not information about this court card under this transit, but a placeholder that happens to mention both.

The gap is entirely a data authoring gap. The entry format in `transitAdaptations.ts` is simple and proven. No structural changes to the engine, the data layer, or any handler are required. The fix is adding transit adaptation entries for court cards.

## Desired State

### Priority order

The highest-priority additions are the four Kings and four Queens under Saturn, Pluto, and Uranus — eight court cards, three planets each, producing 24 high-value entries. These eight cards are the most frequently drawn as representations of real people in a user's life or as dominant inner figures, and they are the cards most likely to carry the weight of outer-planet transits. The Kings and Queens have reached maturity within their elemental domain; they carry authority, and it is authority that Saturn tests, that Pluto excavates, and that Uranus disrupts. When a user draws a King or Queen under one of these three transits, the transit is almost always the interpretively active element in the reading. The current system produces silence where it should produce one of the most specific signals in the deck.

The four Knights under Saturn and Pluto should be the second tier. Knights represent directed energy and pursuit; Saturn confronts them with accountability for where they are charging, and Pluto confronts them with whether the quest they are on is the one that actually matters. Knight entries for Uranus are lower priority because the affinity between Uranus and the Knight's forward-charging nature is well served by the Level 2 element template for Fire and Air suits; it is the Earth and Water knights where the dissonance is most notable.

The four Pages under Saturn are the third tier. Pages are in the first encounter with their elemental domain; Saturn under a Page draw carries a specific meaning about a learning process being formalized or a beginning being made serious, which the element template cannot express.

Pages and Knights under Jupiter, Venus, and Mercury can follow from the established entries for those planets in the existing Major Arcana data; the interpretive patterns there are strong enough to extend.

### Interpretive quality bar for Kings under outer planets

A King of Wands entry under a Saturn transit should name the specific archetypal tension between the King's solar authority — the vision that has become a world, the leader who does not need to shout — and Saturn's demand for accountability and structural integrity. It should say something that could only be said about this particular card under this particular transit: that the visionary leader is being asked whether his world is built on something that will hold, whether the authority he has claimed is earned or merely assumed, whether the fire he has mastered is actually serving the structure or consuming it from within. It should not say that Saturn amplifies Earth energy, because the King of Wands is a Fire card and the tension between those two principles is the entire point.

A King of Cups entry under a Pluto transit should name what Pluto does to emotional mastery: it does not destroy it but excavates what the mastery has been built over. The King of Cups sits calm on turbulent water because he has learned to hold his feelings without being moved off his throne — but Pluto asks what happens when the water beneath the throne itself is what is being disturbed. The entry should name the specific quality of this encounter: not a test of whether the King can feel, but a confrontation with whether his calm is genuine equanimity or something more defended, whether there are feelings beneath the mastery that have never been fully met.

A King of Swords entry under a Uranus transit should name the collision between the King's relationship to law and established truth, and Uranus's disruptive revelation that the framework being applied may need to be overthrown. The King of Swords serves truth rather than himself — but Uranus may be arriving to show him that the framework through which he has been serving truth has become the obstacle to it. This is not a comfortable transit for this card; the entry should say so.

A King of Pentacles entry under a Pluto transit should name what it means for a builder to meet a force that does not respect what has been built. The King of Pentacles has mastered material reality through sustained effort over time; Pluto arrives below the level of what can be managed or controlled. The entry should name the specific quality of this encounter: not the destruction of what the King has built, but the excavation of what his relationship to having-built-it has cost him, what has been organized out of his life in the service of the empire that was built one day at a time.

### Interpretive quality bar for Queens under outer planets

A Queen of Wands entry under a Saturn transit should name the tension between the Queen's confident, magnetic creative leadership and Saturn's demand for sustainable structure. The Queen of Wands leads not by driving others but by being genuinely alive in her own vision — but Saturn asks whether that vision has been built into something that can persist past the moment of inspiration. The entry should name the specific challenge: not a diminishment of her fire but a requirement that the warmth she provides has a floor beneath it, that the invitation she extends is backed by something that won't dissolve the moment the tide of her enthusiasm shifts.

A Queen of Cups entry under a Pluto transit should name what happens when the deepest emotional intelligence is confronted with an excavation at the root level. The Queen of Cups holds depth without drowning; Pluto is the force that goes deeper than she has chosen to go voluntarily. The entry should name what Pluto asks of this figure: not whether she can feel, but whether she has been feeling what is actually there or whether her emotional mastery has been, in part, a way of maintaining the depth at a manageable level, keeping the covered chalice covered.

A Queen of Swords entry under a Saturn transit should name the convergence between her earned clarity and Saturn's demand for honest accounting. This is not a hostile transit; the Queen of Swords is the card of clarity won through surviving something, and Saturn is the planet that rewards exactly that quality. The entry should name the specific quality of this combination: the woman who has lived through difficulty and emerged with precision of perception is now being asked to apply that precision to the structures of her own life, to say clearly what has been built and what it has cost, without the diplomatic softening that even the most clear-eyed people sometimes extend toward their own choices.

A Queen of Pentacles entry under a Pluto transit should name what happens when the most materially grounded nurturer meets a transformational force that operates below the level of what can be tended and managed. The Queen of Pentacles' domain is everything that grows through patient, practical care; Pluto operates below the soil. The entry should name the specific encounter: not a threat to what she has built or nurtured, but a confrontation with whether the tending has been a way of remaining fully present to life or a way of managing it from a safe organizational distance.

### Fallback behavior when no specific adaptation fires

When a court card is drawn under a transit that has no specific adaptation — for example, a Page of Wands under a Neptune transit, or a Knight of Cups under a Sun transit — the system should continue to fall to the Level 2 element-affinity logic. This is acceptable behavior for minor planet combinations. The goal of this enhancement is not to eliminate Level 2 fallback but to eliminate it for the most interpretively significant combinations: outer planets (Saturn, Pluto, Uranus) meeting the mature court figures (Kings and Queens) who carry the most archetypal weight in readings about real people and real authority.

The Level 2 template for court cards under minor planets (Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter) is tolerable because those transits tend to be shorter in duration and lower in interpretive weight. A Venus transit to a King of Cups is well served by noting the Water-element amplification of emotional themes; Venus and the King of Cups are both Water-oriented and the generic alignment sentence is not misleading. It is Saturn-Water versus King of Cups that produces the wrong output — because the tension between Saturn and Water is not a simple amplification but a specific confrontation, and the template currently cannot make that distinction.

### The quality criterion

Each new court card transit adaptation entry should pass the same criterion applied to the existing Major Arcana entries: read it aloud and ask whether any sentence could have been written for a different court card sharing the same element under the same transit. If the answer is yes, the entry has not earned its place. The King of Wands under Saturn entry should be demonstrably wrong if applied to the King of Pentacles under Saturn — and vice versa. The archetypal distinction between these two figures is the entire value the entry provides. If the entry simply names what Saturn does to Fire leadership in general, it belongs at the element-affinity level, not the transit-adaptation level.

The existing entries in `transitAdaptations.ts` demonstrate the standard. The Tower under Pluto entry names specifically what Pluto does to the Tower's structural collapse — it goes deeper than the Tower alone, to dissolution at the root level, the irreversible removal of what has been masking a fundamental imbalance. The Death under Saturn entry names what it means for Saturn to insist on an ending: not punishment but the limit of what discipline can sustain, the arrival of clarity rather than ambiguity. These entries are card-specific first and transit-specific second. The court card entries should meet the same bar.
