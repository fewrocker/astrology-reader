# Steve Jobs: Sprint 0022 — Stop Leaving the Reader Empty-Handed

---

## Where We Are

Sprints 0018 through 0021 built an advance engine that is, honestly, impressive. Combination scoring, couple parity, synastry axis augmentation, LRU caching, category diversity, SR advance preview — all of it is there and it works correctly. The infrastructure story is told.

But here is what I see when I use the product as a user — not as an engineer reviewing changelogs:

I open the Today page. I get a big gold number for my Personal Day, a moon phase emoji, three aspect rows from `getTopActiveTransits`, five energy dots, and a 2–3 sentence GPT paragraph. That paragraph was generated by a function — `getTodayPageInterpretation` in `gptInterpretation.ts` — that receives: the moon phase, three transit aspects, the personal day number, and the archetype label. That is it. The prompt does not know my natal chart. It does not know the numerology personal day *meaning* — just the number and a single-word archetype. It does not know whether the moon is void of course. It does not know that Saturn has been pressing on my Venus for three weeks. It does not know that today is a power configuration according to the advance engine's own scoring.

The result is a paragraph that reads like it was written for anyone born on any day. "Today's energies invite you to reflect inward as the Moon moves through Scorpio..." That could be on any horoscope website in the world. We have a natal chart. We have house placements. We have the personal day archetype's full essence text. We have the advance score. We are sending a thin prompt and getting a thin response, and then we are calling it "Your Morning Synthesis."

That is the core problem for sprint 0022. The reading surfaces the user opens every day are not reading as deeply as the engine underneath them can read. The engine is smart. The surface is not letting the user feel it.

## Issue 1: The Today Page GPT Brief Is Telling the User Almost Nothing Personal

Open `TodayPage.tsx` at line 81. The `getTodayPageInterpretation` call receives four arguments: `currentMoon`, `top` (three transit aspects), `personalDayNum`, and `interpretation?.archetype ?? ''`. That is the entire payload.

Now look at what is *available* at that call site. `chartData` is in scope. `chartData.planets` contains every natal placement — Sun sign and degree, Moon sign, Ascendant. `chartData.houses` contains all 12 house cusps. `interpretation` is the full numerology interpretation object, which has not just the archetype name but `essence`, `keywords`, and `guidance` fields. `moon.isVoid` is computed. The advance score — when it exists — has a `reason` string that already narrates the day's dominant configuration.

None of this reaches the GPT prompt. The function in `gptInterpretation.ts` is called `getTodayPageInterpretation` but it interprets today for an anonymous person who happens to have a moon phase and three transits. It is not interpreting today *for this specific person*.

The fix is precise: extend `getTodayPageInterpretation` to accept the natal chart's Big Three (Sun sign, Moon sign, Ascendant sign), the personal day `essence` sentence (not just the number), the void-of-course flag, and the advance category string when available. Build a richer prompt that tells GPT: "This person has natal Sun in Aries and Moon in Scorpio. Today is Personal Day 7 — The Seeker — a day for introspection and research, not initiation. The Moon is waxing in Gemini, void of course since 2 PM. Saturn is applying a square to their natal Venus in the 3rd house. The advance engine scores today as a Challenging Period." That is a prompt that can produce a paragraph worth reading. That is a paragraph you would actually want to read at 7 AM before deciding how to spend your day.

The `computeTransitAspectBrief` function in `transitAspectBriefs.ts` already generates house-aware sentences for each of the top transit aspects. Those sentences — not just the raw aspect data — should go into the prompt. "Saturn pressing on your Venus in your 3rd house (communication)" is more legible to GPT than "Saturn square Venus, orb 2.3°, challenging, applying."

No new API endpoints. No new GPT types. One function signature extension and a richer prompt string.

## Issue 2: The Transit Timeline Is a Dictionary. The Transit Reading Tab Is a Story. They Should Be the Same Voice.

The Transit Reading tab (the "Reading" tab in `TransitReadingPage.tsx`) uses `AspectRow` with `computeTransitAspectBrief` to show sentences like: "Saturn pressing on your Moon in your 4th house (home and family) — structures built around domestic stability are being examined." That sentence was the product of three sprints of work. It is house-aware. It distinguishes applying from separating. It names the house theme. It uses a specific verb archetype for each planet.

Now tap the Timeline tab. Expand a Saturn square Moon aspect-perfection card. You get: "Emotional stress, internal conflict." That is from `ASPECT_BRIEFS` in `transitEvents.ts` — a flat lookup table indexed by aspect type and natal planet, entirely indifferent to which house Moon is in. It is the same text that appeared in sprint 0010 before any of the house-aware work was done.

The `TimelineEvent` already carries `natalHouse` (line 36 in `TransitTimeline.tsx` reads `event.natalHouse ?? null`). The `event.planet` is the transit planet. The `event.secondPlanet` is the natal planet. The `event.aspectType` is the aspect. Every input that `computeTransitAspectBrief` needs is sitting in the event object. The Timeline is *passing* `natalHouse` to `getPersonalizedEventBrief` but that function ignores it — the lookup is keyed only by aspect type and natal planet.

This is a system with two interpretation paths for the same information. One path is sophisticated. One is a glossary. The user sees both and the product feels inconsistent.

The fix is the one described in the sprint vision: for `aspect-perfection` events, replace the `getPersonalizedEventBrief` call in `EventCard` (lines 35–36 in `TransitTimeline.tsx`) with a call to `computeTransitAspectBrief`. Keep `getPersonalizedEventBrief` for the `brief` field (the one-line summary on the collapsed card), but use `computeTransitAspectBrief` for the `detailText` shown when the card is expanded. The `transitEvents.ts` `ASPECT_BRIEFS` table should be deprecated for aspect-perfection events — it should be the fallback, not the primary source. `transitEvents.ts` retains its role for ingress briefs and station briefs, which have no equivalent in `transitAspectBriefs.ts`.

One quality check: when `natalHouse` is null (no birth time known), `computeTransitAspectBrief` produces a house-blind sentence. That is the correct fallback — still better than the dictionary entry.

## Issue 3: The Synastry Aspect Row Knows There Is a House. It Says Nothing About It.

Expand a synastry aspect row in `SynastryPage.tsx`. You see the `computeSynastryAspectBrief` text. This brief describes the aspect between two planets in abstract terms — Venus trine Mars is harmonious attraction energy, and so on. It is correct and it is useful.

But directly above the aspect list, the House Overlays section tells you that Person B's Venus is in Person A's 5th house. The 5th house is pleasure, creativity, romance, play. That is not a coincidence. That is the house overlay context that *explains why* the Venus-Mars trine feels the way it does between these two people. A Venus-Mars trine when Venus is in the 5th overlay is a very different relational dynamic than a Venus-Mars trine when Venus is in the 8th overlay (transformation, depth, intensity, power).

The user is reading two separate sections that describe the same energy from two different angles, and the product never connects them. The house overlay data is in `synastryData.houseOverlays`. The aspect row knows `a.person1Planet` and `a.person2Planet`. The logic to cross-reference is straightforward: when expanding the aspect row, check whether `person1Planet` appears in the house overlay list for person 2 in a high-signal house (the constants are already defined: `HIGH_SIGNAL_HOUSES = [1, 4, 5, 7, 8, 12]` at line 185 of `SynastryPage.tsx`). If so, render one additional sentence in the expanded panel: "Partner's Venus is in your 5th house — this aspect activates your pleasure and creativity axis."

This is selective. Not every aspect fires a house note. Only when a planet in the aspect also has a house overlay in a relationship-significant house. The sprint vision is correct about this constraint: a mechanical connection on every row becomes noise. Selective means meaningful.

The current `SynastryAspectsSection` component does not receive the `houseOverlays` prop. That is the structural gap. The component needs to be extended to accept overlay data, and the `AspectRow` call or the expanded panel needs to check for the cross-reference. The `computeSynastryAspectBrief` function signature does not need to change — the house note is a separate sentence rendered after the existing brief.

## Code Quality: One Clean-Up That Reduces Fragility

`AdvanceTab.tsx` currently exports `computeCombinedWeight`, `COMBINATION_PLANETS`, `COMBINATION_WEIGHT_THRESHOLD`, and `COMBINATION_WEIGHT_NORMALIZE` for use by `CoupleAdvanceTab`. These scoring primitives live in a file that is primarily a React component — 700+ lines of component logic with engine functions embedded in the middle. `CoupleAdvanceTab` imports them by name from the component file.

This coupling means that any reorganization of `AdvanceTab.tsx` risks breaking the import chain in `CoupleAdvanceTab`, and that the scoring logic cannot be tested or reused without importing a React component file. The scoring functions should be extracted to a dedicated module — call it `src/engine/advanceScoring.ts` — and both `AdvanceTab` and `CoupleAdvanceTab` import from there. The component file imports the engine functions from the engine module, not the other way around.

This is not a major refactor. It is a boundary clarification. The `runAdvancePreCalculation` generic function, `computeCombinedWeight`, `computeEnergyRating` (the legacy one, still used in `TodayPage.tsx` for the energy dots), `scoreSnapshot`, and the scoring constants live in one file. Both tabs import from that file. The component files become thinner. The engine files become more honest about what they contain. This is the kind of code quality improvement that makes the next developer — or the next sprint — faster.

## What I Would Cut or Defer

The sprint vision explicitly excludes advance engine work, Solar Return changes, numerology page changes, and new navigation. I agree with all of these cuts.

I want to name one thing that is tempting but wrong for this sprint: do not add a new "Today's Sky" static block of aspect sentences *above* the GPT morning synthesis on the Today page. The sprint vision proposes this as a way to give the user something to read while GPT loads. I understand the instinct — the GPT load time is real and the blank state is uncomfortable. But adding a static list of three aspect sentences before the GPT paragraph makes the page *longer and more fragmented*, not better. The solution to the blank state during GPT loading is a better skeleton or a better loading message, not additional content. The `GptSkeleton` component already exists. The real fix is making the GPT paragraph worth reading — which is proposal 1 above. Once the paragraph is genuinely personal, users will wait for it.

Do not add more pills to `DailySnapshotCard`. The card already has four pills: moon phase, energy dots, top aspect sentence, and the advance signal badge. Four pills is too many items competing for attention at that scale. If you want to improve the `DailySnapshotCard`, reduce the number of pills — consolidate the energy dots and advance signal into one signal — rather than adding a fifth.

## The Standard for Sprint 0022

The question sprint 0022 must answer: when a user reads the Today page, reads the Transit Timeline, and reads their synastry aspect rows, do they feel like the product *knows* them? Or do they feel like they are reading descriptions of their data?

The engine knows them. The advance scoring knows their natal house placements. The `computeTransitAspectBrief` function knows how Saturn pressing on your Moon in the 4th is different from Saturn pressing on your Moon in the 7th. The synastry house overlay system knows that her Venus is in your 5th house. None of this personalization reaches the surfaces where the user actually reads.

Sprint 0022 is about closing that gap. Not adding new engines. Not new pages. Not new features that require explaining. Making the surfaces that exist read as deeply as the data underneath them can read.

That is the whole sprint.

## Proposals

### Features

**feat-today-gpt-prompt-enrichment** — Extend `getTodayPageInterpretation` in `gptInterpretation.ts` to accept the natal Big Three (Sun/Moon/Ascendant signs), the personal day `essence` text, the `isVoid` flag, and the advance category string (when warm). Pass pre-computed `computeTransitAspectBrief` sentences for the top aspects rather than raw aspect data. The prompt becomes natal-chart-aware and produces a paragraph that is genuinely personal rather than generically daily.

**feat-synastry-aspect-house-note** — Extend `SynastryAspectsSection` in `SynastryPage.tsx` to receive `synastryData.houseOverlays`. When an aspect row is expanded, check whether `person1Planet` or `person2Planet` has a house overlay in a high-signal house (1, 4, 5, 7, 8). If so, render one additional sentence connecting the aspect to the overlay. Selective — only fires on meaningful house contacts, not on every row.

### Issue Fixes

**issue-timeline-aspect-brief-depth** — In `TransitTimeline.tsx`, replace the `getPersonalizedEventBrief` call in `EventCard`'s `detailText` calculation for `aspect-perfection` events with `computeTransitAspectBrief`. The `event.planet` (transit planet), `event.secondPlanet` (natal planet), `event.aspectType`, `event.natalHouse`, and `event.nature` provide all required inputs. This closes the quality gap between the Reading tab and the Timeline tab for the same event.

**issue-today-energy-rating-obsolescence** — The Transit Energy card in `TodayPage.tsx` uses `computeEnergyRating`, the pre-combination-weight formula that the advance engine replaced. When `advanceScore` is non-null (the advance cache is warm for today), suppress the `computeEnergyRating` dots entirely and let the advance category banner — already rendered above — carry the energy signal. The five-dot rating computed from the inferior formula contradicts the advance engine's more accurate assessment of the same sky. Two answers to one question is worse than one good answer.

### Code Enhancements

**code-advance-scoring-extraction** — Extract the advance scoring engine from `AdvanceTab.tsx` into `src/engine/advanceScoring.ts`. Move `computeCombinedWeight`, `computeEnergyRating`, `scoreSnapshot`, `COMBINATION_PLANETS`, `COMBINATION_WEIGHT_THRESHOLD`, `COMBINATION_WEIGHT_NORMALIZE`, `PLANET_WEIGHT`, `STATION_THRESHOLD`, and `runAdvancePreCalculation` to the new file. Both `AdvanceTab.tsx` and `CoupleAdvanceTab.tsx` import from `advanceScoring.ts`. The component files become thinner; the engine boundary becomes explicit.

**code-transit-events-deprecate-aspect-briefs** — After the Timeline fix is in, mark `ASPECT_BRIEFS` in `transitEvents.ts` as deprecated with a comment explaining that `computeTransitAspectBrief` is now authoritative for aspect-perfection events. Retain the object for legacy fallback but ensure no active code path calls it for aspect-perfection events. This is documentation work, not a behavioral change — but it prevents a future contributor from adding new entries to the wrong dictionary.
